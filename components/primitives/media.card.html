Figure & media plate

This card loads base.css and nothing else — the whole figure family lives there, so a marketing page that reaches for it pulls in no app-surface layer. Both panes render the same markup; the dark one carries .lw-band-dark and no rule of its own.

why it is built this way

A screenshot is the one thing on a themed page that cannot re-point. Every other surface reads a role and follows the ground; a raster is pixels, so its white stays white while the page goes navy. The result is a hard slab with square corners bleeding to the edge of the column — and because an image is opaque to axe and to the contrast gate alike, nothing measures it. The plate is the answer the token system can actually give: padding, a role surface, a hairline and two radii.

The inner radius is the line that does the work. A radius on the plate alone leaves the picture's own four square corners sitting inside it, which reads as a slab in a box rather than as a picture. The child rule is > and names the media elements instead of using *, so a plate that also holds a badge or a caption row does not round those.

It is not .lw-browser-frame, and the difference is a claim rather than a style. A toolbar, three dots and an address pill assert that you are looking at a whole application window. Around a crop that is a small lie told by a decoration, which is why this has no bar, no dots, no glow and no variant that adds one. Reach for the browser frame when the image really is a full window; reach for the plate when it is a piece of one.

The surface is --lw-bg-inset, a role, so there is no prefers-color-scheme query anywhere near it — which would be the wrong question in a consumer whose theme is [data-theme] on <html>. On light the mat sits one step below the page; on dark --lw-navy-inset is lighter than --lw-navy-paper, so it sits one step above. Lifted on both grounds, rather than a hole in one of them.
