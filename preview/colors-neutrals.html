Neutrals & navy

The navy anchor and the surface/text scale it grounds. Every hex and ratio below is read back from tokens.css at render time — nothing here is transcribed.

One family, three jobs. navy-700 is sampled from the mark at full saturation and never moves — it is the gradient's dark stop. 900 and deep are the surface tiers, so they step toward the dark theme's own hue and shed chroma: a dark band and a dark card now read as the same colour.

The page, then three lifts. Hairlines sit above them: border-1 for structure, border-2 when a division needs to be felt.

text-1 — headings and primary copy
text-2 — secondary copy, long-form body
text-3 — the muted floor: labels, captions
text-4 — decorative hairline text only

text-4 fails AA as body text and that is intentional. If the number beside it is under 4.5, the token is being used correctly. text-3 at ~4.8 clears AA but has almost no headroom — it is a floor, not a default.

Assessment

navy-700205.2° · 97% S — the mark, unmoved: artwork, gradient, hover navy-900213.0° · 72% S — tooltips, ink buttons, dark bands navy-deep217.0° · 66% S — the full-bleed dark ground, code surface dark paper220.0° · 47% S — --lw-bg on dark surfaces210–220° · 17–23% S — cool grey, same family as the paper
  • The neutrals were already right. Cool grey at 210–220° is the correct temperature beside a blue-navy, the four surfaces stack closely enough not to band, and the text tiers cover every real need with exactly one deliberate sub-AA tier.
  • The navy was two navies, and now it is one. The ramp used to run 205–208° at 95–97% saturation while the dark theme's paper sat at 220°/47% — 14° and ~49 saturation points apart, so a dark band and a dark card did not read as the same system. The ramp now bridges the two: the mark's tier is untouched, and the tiers actually used as grounds step toward the paper's hue and shed chroma. Still deep and unmistakably blue — never slate.
  • Why 700 did not move. It is the stop --lw-gradient-brand feeds and the contrast gate pins at offset 0. Softening it would have desaturated the logo to fix a surface problem — the wrong trade.
  • Nothing lost on contrast. White ink reads 15.75 on navy-900 and 17.94 on navy-deep; the .lw-code comment floor still clears AA at 4.91 and cyan syntax at 7.71. The ramp got calmer, not lighter.
