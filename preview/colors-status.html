Warning is the house amber, separated from the CTA by hue rather than by tier. The fill is — ~6° toward orange from the CTA at full chroma, so it reads as a clean caution and is legible as a different color from the CTA's yellow-amber even side by side. (A mid lightness on a 38° hue reads brown, which is why warning is authored rather than aliased to a cta tier.) The text tier is , on white and on its own tint; on dark, warning text falls back to the amber 400 anchor. Separating by hue is what protects the one-per-view CTA rule.

The fourth state is neutral, not info. Info wants a blue, and every blue here is already spoken for — cyan is the brand and the link color, navy is the ground. Neutral covers what the three alert states can't: idle, pending, draft, not-yet-run — the most common state in a compliance table, and the one that must read as quiet rather than as an alert.

Success and warning are light fills and take navy ink; danger and neutral are dark and take white. That split is measured per fill, not assumed from the family. The last column is the one that bites: a badge is text on the tint, and the lighter -700 shades clear white but only reach ~3.1 on their own tint — which is why the darker -800s are the real floor, and why fill and text are separate tokens.
